The line that splits the whole planet in two is in the wrong place. You see, we slice the Earth into three hundred and sixty meridians, but nothing says zero has to run through England. It could just as well run through Istanbul. It's like the year: nothing in Earth's orbit marks January first. We made it up. England itself started its year on March twenty-fifth for centuries. So who picked Greenwich? Sailors did. For centuries its Royal Observatory guided navigation, and most of the world's charts counted from it. In eighteen eighty-four, delegates met in Washington and voted Greenwich zero. The French objected: "Zero should be neutral, not English!" They abstained, and kept Paris for almost thirty years. Today, tourists line up to straddle a metal line in the courtyard. But that line was set by a telescope aimed with gravity, and gravity there pulls a little crooked. Satellites measure from the center of the Earth instead. Like squaring a picture to a tilted floor. So the real zero runs about a hundred meters east, through the park, where almost nobody stops.
